It might not have taken 2 years to pass peer review [like our other recent publication]({{ site.url }}/2026/08/12/A-slow-review-process-that-paid-off/), but this
was also a relatively long process requiring further wet-lab experiments, as well
as other bureaucratic hurdles outof our control. I am of course talking about Bamu's work
on identifying cis-regulatory variants in the genomes of *E. coli* and *P. aeruginosa*, now published
in [Molecular Systems Biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s44320-026-00245-2).

With respect to [the original preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.10.658842v1) we have repeated the association analysis taking into account
the organization of genes into operons, and we have generated a set of negative control constructs 
(thanks to the help of [Ann Kathrin Heroven from HZI](https://www.helmholtz-hzi.de/forschung/forschungsgruppen/detailseite/molekulare-bakteriologie/#team))
to supplement the experimental validation we had originally presented. Together with other minor changes,
the original message of our work remains valid: a subastantial fraction of variation in gene expression
can be linked to cis-regulatory variants, with an impact on relevant phenotypes such as antimicrobial resistance.

Once again, congratulations to Bamu for her hard work, for being the first PhD student to join the lab,
and for her courage in doing both wet- and dry-lab work!
